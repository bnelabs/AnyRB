# 06b — Primary evidence: recycled-plastic input → finished-product-class result

**Scope:** find peer-reviewed primary (experimental) papers that quantitatively connect a recycled-plastic input (polymer type, batch, mix, filler, minor contamination) to a **finished-product-class** result (modulus, strength, elongation, mass, thickness, sound insulation). Compiled for AnyRB's v0.5 consistency record, which may show a result only when a measurement links the same product and result to a varying input.

**Date:** 2026-10-30 · **author:** researcher-variability · **status:** verified-as-annotated; not a review.

**Grading rules used (strict):**
- `measured on that product class` — the paper physically tests a finished product or a clear product-class specimen (profile/board/lumber/sheet/panel from the product), reports a number that moves with the input, and we read that number from the paper (full text or verifiable table), not from a secondary summary.
- `model only` — the product result is predicted/fitted, not measured.
- `absent` — no qualifying primary study found in this search.
- A plain tensile/flexural coupon of a generic compound is **not** a finished product class and is not graded as one. Literature means, assumed CVs and models are never counted as measured links. The PNAS film result (93 % modulus-variability reduction) is **not** applied to any product here.

**Access note:** ResearchGate/publisher bot walls blocked several full texts; PubMed Central, MDPI and BioResources were fully readable. Rows marked "numbers not verified" are candidates only and are excluded from every "measured" claim. No number below is invented or inferred from a review.

## 2. “Measured on that product class” — what actually qualifies

Only rows **1, 2 and 3** qualify, and each with a qualification that matters for AnyRB:

1. **WPC extruded profile (rHDPE + sawdust, *Polymers* 2024)** is the strongest product-class link found for a *recycled-polymer formulation*: the profile is a real finished extruded WPC product, the test specimens are machined from it, and formulation changes move the result by large, quantified amounts (tensile strength 19.4 → 12.5 MPa; E 1,427 → 2,207 MPa; impact 4.3 → 8.2 kJ/m²; density 1.20–1.34 g/cm³; 24 h water absorption 1.8–2.4 %). It is still a *formulation* link: sawdust %, rHDPE %, coupling agent. It contains **no recycled-batch variation** — one recyclate source — so it cannot support a record where “batch” changes.
2. **WPC boards (rPE + Tetra Pak + wood flour, *BioResources* 2022)** satisfies “clear product-class specimen” most cleanly: real 4 mm boards, 16 specimens per test, standard methods. The input that moved the result is filler type/ratio, and the direction is consistent (strength ↓ with wood flour; modulus ↑; density 942–1017 kg/m³; flexural strength 13.5–19.8 MPa). It is a **formulation** link, again without recyclate batch variation, and it is flat-pressed, not an extruded deck profile.
3. **Full-size recycled-HDPE lumber (*Polymers* 2020)** is the only row where a full-size commercial recycled-plastic product was tested to a product standard (ASTM D6109): E = 0.93 ± 0.03 GPa, Fb = 14.1 ± 0.70 MPa, with a 180-day creep result. But the paper varies **product type** (HDPE lumber vs WPC lumber), not the recyclate. It also does not report the recyclate content/source of the commercial lumber beyond the product datasheet.

**What all three share:** each measures *product-class* response to a *formulation or ageing* change. None measures a product-class response to a *supplied lot/batch* of recyclate. That distinction is the central result of this search.

**Rows 4–8 are deliberately not counted.** Row 4 varies ageing only. Row 5 never made a pipe — it is a coupon study of a pipe compound, so it cannot support a pipe ring-stiffness or pipe-impact result no matter how suggestive its flexural numbers are. Rows 6–8 are title/abstract-level finds whose numbers we could not verify; per the task rules they are candidates, not evidence.

## 3. “Model only” — predictions and claims that are not measurements

- **Creep-life extrapolation (*Polymers* 2020, DOI 10.3390/polym12020262).** The statement that HDPE lumber reaches 3 % outer-fibre strain in ≈1.5 years and the WPC at 30 % Fb in ≈150 years is a power-law extrapolation from 180-day data, not a measured failure. The *measured* part is the 180-day deflection; the 10-year and 1.5/150-year numbers are model only.
- **“Feasibility/commercialisation” claims (e.g. *ACS Omega* 2021 pipe compound).** The paper's conclusion that the blend “was capable of being commercialized in the piping industry” is an inference from coupon tests; no pipe was qualified. Not a measured product link.
- **No paper was found that models a finished-product-class result from a specified recyclate input distribution** (batch means/CVs). The models we found model material coupons, creep coupons, or process rheology; none is a product-class result model that could legitimately be used to fill a record's result.
- **Explicitly rejected as "model only" for AnyRB purposes:** any use of a literature mean, assumed CV, or fitted correlation to predict a product's modulus/strength/sound result for a user's batch. By the record's own rule, that is not a measured link.

## 5. Critical note — what cannot be used

1. **A coupon is not a product.** The pipe-compound study (row 5) and the film/coupon studies are excluded even though their numbers are real. A tensile bar, a flexural coupon or a blend pellet result cannot populate a product-class result in the consistency record.
2. **A formulation–property link is not a batch–property link.** Rows 1–3 show that changing sawdust/filler/coupling or product type moves product-class properties. They do **not** show how much a new recyclate lot moves the same result — the exact question the AnyRB record exists to answer.
3. **Do not copy the PNAS 93 % modulus-variability result** (or its ≥68 % strain figure) to any product in this file. That study is a nacre-inspired composite on rHDPE platelets/PDMS, not a deck board, pipe, panel, lumber or sheet, and its result is for a designed architecture, not a material/product-class link.
4. **Do not use literature means or assumed CVs as the "measured link".** The measured link in AnyRB must be a same-product, same-result measurement with at least two input states. None of the rows above supplies that for a *recyclate batch* input.
5. **Do not treat "measured on that product class" rows as transferable across products.** WPC profile values (row 1) and WPC flat-pressed board values (row 2) differ by a factor of ~2 in modulus and differ in strength direction; they cannot be interpolated to a deck board, pipe or sheet.
6. **Sample size and coverage limits.** Qualified rows rest on n = 5–16 specimens, typically one production batch and one recycler source, with no repeat-lot design. They cannot estimate lot-to-lot variance; variances quoted inside them (e.g. ±1.68 MPa) are within-batch specimen scatter, not batch scatter.
7. **Direction-of-effect caution.** In row 1 the “optimised” formulation has *lower* tensile strength than the initial one while E and impact rise; in row 2 wood flour lowers strength but raises modulus. Both are plausible but they show that the sign of the effect depends on the property and the formulation — another reason not to infer a link from polymer identity or filler content alone.
8. **Access-limited candidates must stay candidates.** Rows 6–8 and the acoustic paper are not evidence until full texts are read; do not quote their titles as if they were results.
9. **Regulatory/product standards context is not a link.** EN 15534-4 / EAD 190005-00-0402 (decking), ASTM D7032, ASTM D6109, ISO 9969 (pipe ring stiffness), ISO 10534-2 (impedance tube) tell you *what to measure* for a product class; they do not supply a recycled-input→result number.

## 6. What this means for AnyRB (decision)

- The literature **does not** contain the kind of measured link the v0.5 record needs: a product-class result measured against a varying *recyclate input* (lot, batch, mix, contamination). What exists is (a) formulation/filler links on product-class specimens and (b) full-size product tests of single commercial recyclate products. This validates the product decision to leave the result **blank** unless the user (or supplier) supplies a same-product measurement — a literature prior cannot honestly fill it.
- The best use of this dossier inside AnyRB is not to populate results but to justify **which product classes have plausible measurement routes and which standards apply** (ASTM D6109/D790/D638 for lumber/WPC coupons; ISO 9969 for pipe; ISO 10534-2 for acoustic panels; EN 15534-4 for decking), and to show that formulation changes of a few tens of percent can move product-class strength by ~30–55 % and modulus by ~50–100 % (rows 1–3) — a measured effect size for the *filler/mix* field, not for the *batch* field.
- The strongest qualified, quantified product-class facts for a demo tooltip are: WPC board flexural strength 13.5–19.8 MPa (formulation range, n = 16; BioResources 2022); full-size recycled-HDPE lumber E = 0.93 ± 0.03 GPa / Fb = 14.1 ± 0.70 MPa (n = 5; Polymers 2020); and WPC profile E = 1,427–2,207 MPa / tensile 12.5–19.4 MPa (n = 5/formulation; Polymers 2024). All three must carry the label *formulation links, not batch links*.
