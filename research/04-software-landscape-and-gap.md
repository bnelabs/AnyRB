# 04 — Software landscape, gap, standards demand, and product position

**Scope:** who already sells software for the pieces AnyRB couples, what those tools actually do (checked against public docs), where the integration gap is, and which regulatory change makes it commercially interesting.
**Method:** public product docs, release notes, standards bodies, public procurement records, trade press; accessed 2026-10-29. Grades: **A** peer-reviewed quantitative; **B** peer-reviewed qualitative; **C** standards/agency/industry document; **D** vendor marketing/trade press; **E** analyst inference (not a source). **†** = inferred from architecture, not documented. Prices only where a public anchor exists; otherwise "not public". Citations `[Rxx]` resolve in `refs-04.json`.

**Verdict.** Every *component* of the AnyRB chain exists commercially: implicit-model latices (nTop, Carbon, Altair/Gen3D, Hyperganic, Materialise), generic uncertainty quantification (optiSLang, HyperStudy, Isight, Dakota), multiscale material modelling with a 2025 UQ workflow aimed at recycled short-fibre compounds (Cadence Digimat, ex-Hexagon/MSC) [R31][R32][R33], materials data management (Granta MI) [R36], and lot-genealogy MES (Opcenter) [R27][R28]. What does not exist as a product is the *closed loop*: recyclate lot distribution → structural reliability → internal-geometry optimisation → per-batch print recipe → auditable qualification artifact, priced for the small and mid-sized users who print recycled polymer. The closest single competitor is Cadence Digimat; its architecture (coupon-calibrated material models plus generic CAE, sold quote-only to automotive Tier-1s) makes our segment structurally unattractive to it. The defensible asset is therefore not the solver but the lot-property dataset, the workflow and the artifact.

All of these are **deterministic-material tools**: the public documentation reviewed shows no lot-to-lot distribution as an input, no reliability target as an optimisation constraint, no batch compensation as an output. That is absence of evidence, not proof of absence.

### 1.2 Simulation, optimisation, UQ

**Altair OptiStruct/HyperStudy** does topology/size optimisation with DOE and stochastic drivers [R20][R21]. **Ansys Mechanical** is the structural solver [R22]; **optiSLang** adds UQ, robustness/reliability, robust design optimisation and sensitivity around arbitrary CAE models [R18][R19] — a *generic* engine: it accepts a distribution the user supplies but brings no recyclate data, no lot model, no design artifact. **COMSOL** is a general PDE platform usable for homogenisation and coupled physics [R23]; university seat ≈ US$4.5k perpetual base plus US$0.6–4.5k per module and ~20 % maintenance [R24][R25] (C; no public list). **Dassault Abaqus + Isight** offers design exploration with stochastic/Monte-Carlo methods [R29][R30]. **Siemens NX/Simcenter 3D** provides CAD/CAE optimisation [R26]. **Cadence Digimat** is treated separately (§1.4) because it is the closest competitor.

### 1.3 Material data platforms

**Ansys Granta MI** manages enterprise materials data: test-data capture, version control, CAD/CAE/PLM integration, AM template, 250k+ reference dataset, ML and a sustainability add-on [R36]. It stores many datasets per material but does not propagate a distribution to a reliability calculation or a print recipe. **CAMPUS** offers free, standardised resin datasheets [R37]; **UL Prospector** gives free material search including UL Yellow Card (Recognized Component) data [R38][R39]; **MatWeb** is a free property database [R40]. Recycled-material variability is an explicit UL webinar topic, with Digimat calibration demonstrated [R35] — a signal that the data layer already senses the problem.

### 1.4 Closest competitor: Digimat

Digimat (MSC/Hexagon, now **Cadence**, acquisition completed by early 2026 [R34]) is a multiscale material-modelling platform: mean-field and FE homogenisation, process simulation, virtual allowables, material-card management. Two facts matter. First, **Digimat 2025.1 shipped a UQ workflow** for short-fibre-reinforced plastics: variability in material properties and manufacturing process → structural reliability, accelerated by ML/ROM [R31]. Second, its public 2025 conference material is titled *"Improving reliability in the design of recycled short-fibre-reinforced plastics"* and shows Monte-Carlo/ROM failure probability for recycled compounds [R32]; a 2026 vendor success story has RadiciGroup using the UQ workflow on recycled PA66-GF30 (Renycle) to compute failure probability under torque loading [R33] (D — vendor narrative, not independent validation).

So an incumbent has already connected *material/process variability → part reliability* for recycled compounds. The differences are architectural:

- Its input is a **calibrated material model** (fibre orientation, process simulation, coupon tests), not a **supplier lot passport** (MFI/IV/density/tensile) or lot time-series.
- UQ is a **study around a fixed part**, not a **design loop** re-optimising internal geometry against reliability across a lot distribution.
- Output is a **CAE result**, not a per-batch **print recipe/compensation**, not a self-contained **qualification report**.
- Sold **quote-only** to expert CAE users; its public recycled-material evidence is injection-moulded short-fibre, not extrusion-printed unfilled recyclate latices [R31][R32][R33].

### 1.5 MES/QMS and sorting

**Siemens Opcenter** bundles MES, APS, quality management/CAQ and LIMS with lot-level genealogy and lab sampling [R27][R28]; PTC ThingWorx and AVEVA occupy the same industrial layer (†). These record what happened to a lot; they do not design for it and have no structural reliability model. On the feedstock side, **Tomra AUTOSORT** uses NIR+VIS (plus X-ray options) to identify polymer type [R41], and Pellenc ST and Recycleye sell optical/AI sorting [R44][R45]; the EU **PolyCE** project published design-for-recycling and PCR-use guidelines [R42][R43]. These are potential **data and channel partners**: sorting generates lot-level composition data, exactly the input AnyRB needs. No public evidence any sorter vendor offers design-side reliability software.

### 1.6 Open-source stack

FEniCS [R51], CalculiX [R52] and code_aster [R53] provide FE; Dakota [R46], OpenTURNS [R47], UQLab [R48], chaospy [R49] and SALib [R50] provide UQ/sensitivity; NLopt, BoTorch, PyTorch and DTU's academic TopOpt codes provide optimisation and ML [R54][R55][R70]. The stack can reproduce AnyRB's *mathematics* for free; it cannot reproduce the workflow, the curated lot distributions, the non-expert defaults, or the report. AnyRB is therefore "open-source components, commercial product" — and must say so: the moat is data and usability, not algorithms.

**Reading the matrix.** Three columns do the work: *lot-distribution input*, *batch-adaptive recipe*, *qualification report*. Nobody ticks all three; only AnyRB plans to. Geometry tools tick lattice and STL but assume one material state. MES ticks lineage but has no mechanics. AnyRB does not out-solve anyone; it is the only product where the five steps are one workflow with recyclate defaults.

## 3. The gap, stated precisely

The claim is **not** "nobody does UQ on recycled materials". It is:

> No commercial product couples **(1) a recyclate lot-property distribution derived from supplier proxies → (2) a structural reliability target → (3) optimisation of internal geometry → (4) a per-batch process/print recipe for the lot actually received → (5) an auditable virtual qualification report**, for users who are not CAE experts and cannot afford a six-figure material-modelling platform.

Why the incumbents' architectures make this hard:

1. **Fixed-part, not fixed-batch.** CAE UQ perturbs inputs around one model; the output is a probability about *a part*. AnyRB's optimisation variable is the *geometry* and its output is a design rule plus a lot decision — two different users and two different products.
2. **Material card, not population.** Granta stores test records [R36]; Digimat stores calibrated models [R31]. Neither publishes a lot/within-lot variance model or a path from MFI/IV/density to mechanical distributions.
3. **Unit economics exclude the segment.** Quote-only, expert-deployed, seat-based platforms are priced for Tier-1 programmes. A compounder, filament maker or furniture brand with 3–10 recyclate SKUs and no CAE team is not a serviceable account.
4. **Output is a study, not a decision artifact.** PPAP-style approval needs a compact report with assumptions, inputs and a pass/fail rule — not a 60-page analyst CAE report.

**What AnyRB is not:** not a better FEA solver, not a Digimat replacement for injection-moulded short-fibre parts, not a certification body. v0.1 is a linear-elastic 2D frame idealisation; Digimat's physics is far ahead. The wedge is workflow, data and price, and the V&V/claim-limits discipline is what keeps it honest.

**Honest reading:** regulation creates demand for *evidence*, not simulation. Recycled content is verified by audit and test (EN 15343, UL, PPAP); simulation is accepted where risk is understood and credibility demonstrated. AnyRB sells decision support and documentation — and its report must say so loudly.

## 5. Go-to-market sketch

**Beachhead 1 — recycled-filament producers and polymer print farms.** They buy recyclate lots, see the drift directly, have little test budget, and a failed batch is a customer complaint. Offer: "design for the filament lots you actually get", lot passport, per-lot print recipe. Cheapest acquisition, best data contribution.

**Beachhead 2 — contract furniture, houseware and consumer-goods brands under recycled-content commitments.** They must raise recycled content (or claim it) while holding durability and warranty, and do not own CAE seats. Deliverable: design-review artifact — "this lattice, in this recyclate population, meets the reliability target; here is the acceptance window for incoming lots". Higher willingness to pay.

**Beachhead 3 — custom AM service bureaus and design studios** producing lattice parts in recyclate. They quote per part and need fast feasibility ("can this print in rPETG lots at ≥99% reliability?"). They resell the report to their customers — a pull channel.

*Later:* compounders (data partner + OEM engine licence), packaging converters, automotive Tier-2/Tier-3 once data and credibility exist.

**Pricing hypotheses (E, to be tested):**

| Offer | Price hypothesis | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Free/OSS engine + presets | 0 | free open-source stack [R46]–[R55] |
| Studio seat (lot library, report export) | US$3–6k/yr | below nTop procurement anchor [R03]; near Magics-lattice indicative [R11] |
| Per-qualification-report credits | US$300–1,500 | priced to a customer approval, not a seat |
| Data partnership (free seats for anonymised lots) | 0 + revenue share | builds the moat |
| OEM/embedded licence | US$25–60k/yr | one integration, many parts |

**Deployment:** local-first, network-free desktop app (as the demo) for data sovereignty; optional web studio; Grasshopper/Rhino and Fusion plugin path; MES/LIMS API later. **Data moat:** lot-property database linking cheap proxies (MFI/IV/density/NIR) → mechanical distributions → design outcomes, with hierarchical priors per recyclate stream. Incumbents' material-card model does not generate this, and recyclate lot series are not in their customer base.

**Three-year wedge (rough, E):** Y1 — 5–10 design partners across the three segments, 200–1,000 lots, one physical validation study with a lab/university, priced pilots. Y2 — 30–60 paid seats, first reports used inside PPAP/APQP packs, one compounder/sorter partnership, 5,000 lots. Y3 — 100–200 seats, US$1–3M ARR potential, OEM-embedded engine, published V&V benchmark, 25k+ lots. Planning numbers, not forecasts.

## 6. Honest risks

1. **Incumbent adds UQ to geometry tools.** Digimat already pairs UQ with recycled SFRP [R31]–[R33]; nTop/Carbon/Altair could add a material-variability field. Trigger: any of them shipping a lot-distribution input. Mitigation: own the lot data and segment, stay cheap, integrate rather than out-physics.
2. **Data scarcity/provenance.** Recyclate lot data is proprietary, irregular and undocumented; literature proxies may not transfer to a user's stream. Trigger: pilot where predicted and measured distributions disagree. Mitigation: user calibration, explicit prior uncertainty, validation studies, no hidden model error.
3. **Liability.** A "qualification report" can be read as certification; a field failure could be attributed to it. Mitigation: terms, claim limits, decision-support language, insurance at revenue.
4. **Willingness to pay.** Beachhead 1 is price-sensitive; recycled-content compliance may be met by buying certified recyclate alone. Trigger: pilots convert to free usage. Mitigation: per-report pricing, land via service bureaus, prove avoided scrap/rework.
5. **Recyclate market dynamics.** Recyclate-virgin price spreads are volatile; PPWR/ELV delays soften urgency. Mitigation: segments with contractual recycled content and existing variance cost.
6. **Physics credibility.** v0.1 ignores plasticity, creep, fatigue, layer-adhesion anisotropy and print defects; users may over-trust a clean number. Mitigation: visible limitations, sensitivity display, V&V roadmap, never present simulated reliability as measured.
7. **Standards inertia.** PPAP, UL and EN 15343 stay physical/audit-based; simulation is supplementary for years. Caps the product's role — and is why incumbents will not build the workflow soon.
8. **Consolidation and bundling.** Siemens absorbed Altair [R05]; Cadence absorbed Hexagon D&E [R34]. CAE is bundling, raising the bar for standalone tools while validating the workflow layer as the battleground.

## 7. What we could not verify

- **nTop list pricing** — no public price; only a UKRI procurement record [R03] and third-party estimates (D).
- **Carbon Design Engine current pricing** — 2021 trade-press figures [R04]; unverified for 2026.
