# AnyRB — Demo & First-Impression / UX Judgement (Demo, 25%)

**Score: 5.0 / 10**

**One-line verdict:** The live app contains one genuinely product-like moment — a
plain-language decision headline that replaces the page title — but the artefact a
judge is asked to score is, by the team's own docs, an unfinished draft video
(synthetic voice, 2:42 not 3:00, ~60 s of near-static screen), wrapped around a
first-open that is a long blank form with no dominant next action and no visual
representation of the one idea the product is about: a swing versus a target window.

This is fixable without touching the engine. A finished founder-voiced recut,
a sticky decision, and one honest range bar would move this to ~7.5. As
submitted today it reads as a well-written engineering tool that has not yet
been turned into a demo.

### The synthetic-narration issue

This is disclosed, to the team's credit — three times:

- `DEMO_SCRIPT.md:3` — "A **draft screen recording with synthetic narration** …
  not the final submission video. Replace the narration with your own voice,
  and re-record the failure case if you want a full 3:00 cut."
- `demo/README.md:26–28` — "pacing reference only: replace the voice with the
  founder's before submitting"
- `SUBMISSION.md` — "Draft reference cut … (synthetic narration; replace before
  submission)", and the checklist items for public repo / live URL / video link
  are all still unset or unchecked.

Honest disclosure in the repo does not help the person scoring the Demo
category, who watches the artefact in front of them. A synthetic voice over a
screen recording is the single easiest thing for a hackathon judge to pattern-
match as low-effort, and here it compounds: because the voice is not the
founder's, none of the script's best lines ("Recycled plastic is not a
datasheet. It is a distribution.", `DEMO_SCRIPT.md:99–100`) carry any
first-person credibility — there is no "I run this shop / I measured this
batch" moment anywhere in the pack. **If this file is what gets linked, Demo
is capped in the ~5 range no matter how good the live app is.** Score
accordingly, and treat the recut in §7 as P0, not polish.

**Video sub-score: 4 / 10** (coherent flow and one strong decision moment;
unfinished by admission, 18 s short, ~60 s of static holds, no captions, no
close, no URL, low bitrate for text).

## 2. First-open experience — the first 10 seconds

Reconstructing from `screenshots/v10/first-open-blank.png` (1440×1227,
2 Nov — the current blank build), `app/index.html:17–43` and
`app/main.js:24` (`normalizeState({ blank: true })`):

What the judge sees, in order:

1. `AnyRB` (17 px) top-left, and top-right in 13 px grey: "From this delivery
   to a build that holds" (`index.html:12–15`). Fine, quiet, forgettable.
2. A very large question — clamp(42px, 6.4vw, 70px) (`styles.css:78–85`):
   **"What do you want to make?"**
3. One support sentence: "Name the part, set one or more properties that have
   to hold, and describe this delivery." (`index.html:20`)
4. A brief textarea whose **placeholder is the entire sample brief** — a
   60-word paragraph in light grey (`index.html:27`, placeholder colour
   #aaa7a0 at `styles.css:515`). To a first-time viewer this reads as
   pre-filled content that is somehow greyed out, not as an invitation.
5. Three small, underlined, text-only actions at 12–13 px: "Use a sample
   brief" (`styles.css:484–492`), "Read with Meta", "Speak"
   (`styles.css:518–551`). None is a filled button. The only filled button in
   the design system, `.pill` (`styles.css:96–110`), is the **Copy build**
   button — and it is `hidden` until a build already passes
   (`index.html:42`, `main.js:330`).
6. An "Examples" row — Bracket / Slat / Crate / Planter / Pallet block /
   Something else — styled as underlined text links, not selectable cards
   (`index.html:33–41`, `styles.css:404–416`).
7. Below the fold: two columns headed "Engineering" and "Batch" containing,
   in total, **18+ empty inputs** (counted from `index.html:49–133`: product,
   mode, span, width, thickness, property, low, high, plastic type, batch,
   mix, dirt level, dirt text, stiffness low/high, strength low/high, density,
   plus steadier-stream fields). The decision-adjacent panels — Builds, Batch
   gap, Ask Meta — are all `hidden` until the state is "ready"
   (`main.js:333–335`), so the empty state offers **no preview of what success
   looks like, no checklist of what's missing, and no disabled-but-visible
   result area**.

So in the first 10 seconds the judge must *infer* the intended path. There are
three plausible first clicks (sample brief, a Crate example, typing in the
textarea) and none is visually primary. The fastest path is genuinely good
once found — "Use a sample brief" fills *and* submits in one click
(`main.js:864–871`) — but it is one of the smallest elements on the page.

The blank start itself is a principled choice and the script defends it well
("No preset product", `DEMO_SCRIPT.md:27–30`): it proves the tool is not a
hard-coded bracket demo. But "no assumed product" has been implemented as
"no guidance". A blank state can still teach: e.g. a three-line readiness
list ("1 · Part 2 · What must hold 3 · This delivery — you can start with the
sample brief") occupying the space where Builds will appear. Right now a
judge who doesn't read the placeholder paragraph has no idea the sample brief
exists as a one-click path.

**Stale-screenshot problem in the checked pack.** `demo/README.md:15–22`
describes `first-open.png` as the empty first open and `mobile.png` as "the
same first open at 390 px". OCR + field values say otherwise for those two
files (both dated 31 Oct, predating the blank build): `mobile.png`
(780×4376) shows Batch pre-filled with the old defaults — stiffness 700/850,
strength 14/18, density 0.94, "stiffer bottle stream" 2000/40, mode "Fixed at
one end" — i.e. `DEFAULT_STATE` (`product.js:64–92`), not the blank state in
`main.js:24`. The genuinely blank screenshot is `first-open-blank.png`
(2 Nov), which the README never mentions. A judge cross-checking the demo
pack against the live app will find the pack contradicting its own caption.
`brief-verifier.png` (2880×1800, 2 Nov) is current but is a scrolled crop —
it starts mid-form at "What has to hold" and never shows the decision
headline it is meant to evidence. And there is **no current (v10) failure-case
screenshot at all**: the only failure screenshot in the tree is
`screenshots/v8/wide-swing.png` ("Narrow the strength swing."), which
`demo/README.md:40–44` explicitly retires as "not part of the judged demo".

**First-open sub-score: 5 / 10** (memorable question, honest blank start,
one-click sample path exists — but no primary action, no guidance, no
preview, and the screenshot pack mislabels its own evidence).

## 3. Time-to-value

Two paths, very different clocks:

**Sample-brief path (the demo path).** Click "Use a sample brief" →
`readBrief()` POSTs to `/api/product/brief` (30 s abort timeout,
`main.js:796–797`) → fields are painted → `refresh()` evaluates locally and
debounces a proposals call by 850 ms (`main.js:363`). The local mechanics
result is effectively immediate once state exists; in the recorded cut the
decision headline is on screen ~10 s after the brief is submitted (frames
~30 s → ~40 s). If the Meta endpoint is slow or down, the brief path fails
softly: "Muse Spark is unavailable. Fill the fields below; the local
mechanics still works." (`main.js:818`) — honest, but it routes the judge
straight into the slow path. Note also `HACKATHON.md:96`: "sample brief
parses (cached in the demo)" — the recorded time-to-value is partly a cached
path; a judge typing a *novel* brief live may see a slower, less certain
version of this moment. The demo should say so or show one uncached parse.

**Manual path (the "real use" path).** Fill 18+ fields, several of which a
small-shop user plausibly does not know offhand (stiffness/strength in MPa,
density in g/cm³). There is no unit help, no "typical rHDPE" hint, no
progressive disclosure — the Batch column asks for stiffness low *and* high,
strength low *and* high, before the user has seen a single output. Every
keystroke triggers a full re-evaluation and a debounced model call
(`main.js:370–373`). Until `isReady()` passes (`product.js:227 ff.`,
`isReady` via `main.js:358`), the headline just sits at "What do you want to
make?" with no indication of which fields are still blocking.

Net: time-to-value is ~10 seconds if you already know to click the small
sample-brief link, and several minutes of form-filling if you don't.
That spread is a UX choice, not a technology limit, and the video hides it
by never showing the manual path at all.

**Time-to-value sub-score: 6 / 10** on the demo path, 3 / 10 manual;
blended **5 / 10**.

## 4. Calculator/form sheet vs. a product that gives a decision

This is the most interesting tension in the app, because it is genuinely
both — in sequence.

**Before ready: it is a form sheet.** Two labelled columns of bordered
inputs (44 px, 10 px radius, `styles.css:160–171`), underlined text buttons,
section notes ("Change the build, not the plastic." / "Measured swing sets
the build. Dirt tells us what to fix." — those two lines are the best copy
in the form, `index.html:48,101`). Every affordance says "spreadsheet with
better typography".

**After ready: it becomes a decision product, and this is real.** The H1 —
the largest type on the page — is *replaced* by the decision
(`main.js:336–339` → `decisionFor`, `product.js:855–933`):

- Pass: **"Make the 400 mm crate side, 60 × 15 mm."** / "Blend 20% of …,
  then make the …" (`product.js:906–908`)
- Fail: **"Narrow the strength swing."**, **"Lift the strength low end by
  … MPa."**, **"Bring the … high end down by …"** (`product.js:881–883`)
- Support sentence states the achieved range against the required range in
  plain verbs: "This build **carries** 95.4–111 kg and **weighs** 338.4 g —
  inside your Strength 95–115 kg; Weight 320–360 g." (`product.js:866–874`,
  confirmed in video frame ~40 s and `brief-verifier.png`).
- The primary button becomes the decision's own verb: **Copy build** (with a
  formatted plain-text plan, `product.js:919–931`) or **Ask Meta** on failure
  (`product.js:900`). The button label *is* the next action. That is
  product thinking, not calculator thinking.

**Why it still lands as "calculator with a good headline":**

1. **The decision is not sticky and not summarised in place.** The headline
   lives only at the top. The user edits fields 600–1200 px below it; on
   every change it cross-fades (`main.js:336`, `styles.css:354–358`) while
   off-screen. When a user down in the Batch column breaks the build, the
   only local signals are small paragraphs appearing under the Batch form
   (see §6). There is no persistent decision bar, no status dot, no
   "passing / not passing" indicator near the edit.
2. **"Builds that pass" is a text table, not a comparison.** Rows are
   move / size / range-as-sentence / added-mass (`main.js:189–213`,
   `proposalRow` at `product.js:935–954`), winner marked only by weight-600
   type and an 11 px grey "least build mass" tag (`styles.css:257–264`).
   Two passing builds (current 60×15 vs. wider 61×15) cannot be compared at
   a glance — the eye must parse "carries 95.4–111 kg · weighs 338.4 g" vs.
   "carries 97–112.9 kg · weighs 344 g" and do the subtraction.
3. **The gap explanation repeats the headline at lower fidelity.** The same
   "needs X; delivery gives Y" sentence appears in the support line, then
   again per-property in the Batch gap panel (`main.js:254–259`). In
   `brief-verifier.png` the gap panel is also visibly confusing: "Strength:
   this build needs 41.4–50.1 MPa; your delivery gives 41.6–48.4 MPa. It
   fits." — a judge doing the interval arithmetic will notice the needed
   window (41.4–50.1) is *wider* than the delivery window and ask why "it
   fits" is asserted at 1-decimal rounding; and the weight line converts the
   requirement into a density window ("needs 0.9–1 g/cm³; your delivery gives
   0.9–0.9 g/cm³") which is meaningless to a shop user and is an artefact of
   `formatRange` rounding to 1 decimal (`main.js:298–302`).

**Decision-product sub-score: 6 / 10** — the headline mechanism is a 8/10
idea executed at 5/10 persistence.

## 5. Visual hierarchy and the missing range visualisation

**Hierarchy.** Type scale is confident (70 px H1 → 19 px support → 15 px
section heads → 12 px labels) and the palette is restrained near-black on
off-white (`styles.css:1–8`). But *action* hierarchy is inverted: the
elements that move you forward (Read with Meta, Speak, sample brief, Ask)
are the smallest, palest things on the page (12–13 px, grey or
border-bottom only, `styles.css:335–344,484–551`), while the element that
does nothing until the end (the form) occupies everything. Pass/fail has
almost no visual encoding anywhere: the only status colours in the entire
stylesheet are 11 px accepted/rejected labels in the trace (#2c6b3f /
#8a4b2f, `styles.css:461–462`). A passing decision and a failing decision
are the same black 70 px type. The winner row differs from the runner-up by
font weight. For a tool whose entire output is "this fits / this doesn't,
by this much", the absence of any at-a-glance status encoding is the
central visual-design gap.

**Range visualisation: there is none. This is the single biggest product
miss.** Confirmed by search: no `<canvas>`, no SVG chart, no bar element in
`app/index.html` / `app/main.js` / `app/styles.css` / `app/product.js` (the
only SVG in the app is the favicon, `index.html:7`). Every range in the
product — the delivery swing, the target window, the achieved build range,
the material gap — is rendered as numbers inside sentences:

- "carries 95.4–111 kg · weighs 338.4 g" (build rows)
- "Strength: needs 56.7–65.4 MPa; delivery gives 41.6–48.4 MPa." (gap)

The product's thesis, in the script's own closing line, is "Recycled plastic
is not a datasheet. It is a distribution." (`DEMO_SCRIPT.md:99`). A
distribution that is never drawn. A judge must perform mental interval
arithmetic — is 95.4–111 inside 95–115? by how much at the tight end?
(Answer: 0.4 kg of margin at the low end — arguably the most important
number in the whole demo, and it appears nowhere; it has to be subtracted
in your head.) The Batch gap in the failure case — delivery 41.6–48.4
against needed 56.7–65.4 — is a picture begging to be two bars that don't
overlap. One horizontal interval chart per requirement (target window as a
band, delivery/build range as a bar on the same axis, margin labelled) is
the difference between "I read that it fits" and "I *see* that it fits".
This is also the screenshot a judge would remember and a Devpost thumbnail
could carry; today every screenshot is a wall of form fields.

**Hierarchy/visualisation sub-score: 3 / 10.**

## 6. Mobile and the failure case

**Mobile** (`styles.css:360–389,443–447,533–555`; `screenshots/v10/mobile.png`
780×4376 — noting the staleness caveat from §2, layout verified against
current CSS):

- Genuinely responsive: one column under 900 px, span/width/thickness stack
  under 760 px, top tagline hidden, and — the best mobile decision in the
  app — the primary button becomes a fixed bottom bar (52 px, full width,
  `styles.css:371–381`). Copy build is thumb-reachable at all times.
- But the page is ~4,400 px tall at 390 px wide, and the *result* is split
  across it: decision headline at the top, Builds inside Engineering
  (mid-page), Batch gap after the entire Batch form (§4 problem, amplified —
  the DOM order `index.html:75–83` vs. `:135` puts the explanation a full
  form away from the list it explains). On mobile, changing a Batch field
  and seeing what it did to the decision means scrolling the full height of
  the page. No sticky summary mitigates this.
- Brief actions stack vertically and left-align as small text links
  (`styles.css:534–536,554`) — Read with Meta / Speak are easy to miss and
  small tap targets (~13 px text, no padding block) on the device where
  voice input would be most valuable.
- The video has no phone shot despite `DEMO_SCRIPT.md:14` listing one as
  optional; the only mobile evidence is the (stale) screenshot.

**Mobile sub-score: 6 / 10** (structurally sound, fixed CTA; result
fragmentation and tap-target size unaddressed).

**Failure case.**

In the app, failure is handled better than most hackathon entries handle
success — the decision headline becomes an instruction ("Narrow the strength
swing." etc., `product.js:881–883`), the primary button becomes "Ask Meta"
(`product.js:900`), the empty state under Builds says "No build passes yet.
The batch gap is below." (`index.html:81`) and the gap panel states needed
vs. delivered per property with a material action (`main.js:241–259`). It
never says "don't use this delivery", consistent with `README.md:40–42`.

Visibility, however, is weak at exactly the moment of failure:

- The headline changes **at the top of the page while the user is editing
  at the bottom**. Nothing scrolls, sticks, or flashes. The 160 ms opacity
  fade (`styles.css:354–358`) is invisible off-screen. There is no colour,
  badge, or icon change anywhere near the edited field.
- "No build passes yet." is 14 px muted text (`styles.css:266–270`), visually
  quieter than the form labels around it.
- The gap panel lives in the *other column* on desktop / far below on
  mobile (§6), so "The batch gap is below" (`index.html:81`) is, on mobile,
  not really below — it is a screen and a half away.
- In the video, as detailed in §1, the failure gets ~20–30 s, its headline
  is scrolled out of frame, and its Ask Meta answer and recovery are never
  shown. In the screenshot pack it has no current image at all. **The case
  the script calls the important one is the least visible case in every
  artefact provided.**

**Failure-visibility sub-score: 5 / 10** (logic and copy: strong;
placement, signalling, and demo coverage: weak).
