# AnyRB — Judge report: Impact (30%)

**Score: 5.5 / 10**

**One-line verdict:** The pain is real, evidenced, and specific, and the product genuinely converts it into a decision instead of a rejection — but no real beneficiary has ever been shown using it, the flagship case is assembled from two different published sources and already passes, and the app surfaces engineering numbers while leaving the actual consequences (a wasted delivery, over-design cost, blend cost) invisible on screen. A judge who only watches the product for three minutes sees a competent range-checker; the impact story lives in the narration around it.

Evaluated read-only against `README.md`, `HACKATHON.md`, `V0_8_SPEC.md`, `data/CASE_STUDY.md`, `app/index.html`, `app/product.js`, `app/main.js`, `DEMO_SCRIPT.md`, and `research/` (notably dossiers 00, 01, 04). No market size, customer, or saving is credited anywhere below unless the repo itself evidences it.

## Strongest impact argument already present

This is the best thing in the submission, and it is genuinely good:

1. **The problem is a distribution, and the repo proves the distribution is large.** One supplier's rHDPE plates measured 1,350 ± 239 MPa (CV 17.7%, n=50) while a literature compilation sits at 1,213 ± 654 MPa (CV ≈54%) and modulus spans 380–1,250 MPa across three US MRFs ([research/01-recyclate-variability.md:29](../research/01-recyclate-variability.md#L29), summarised in [research/00-executive-evidence-map.md:9](../research/00-executive-evidence-map.md#L9)). Ten PCR-PP lots met the same MFR/density spec yet ranged 2–24 wt% filler and 10–100% elongation ([research/01-recyclate-variability.md:30](../research/01-recyclate-variability.md#L30)). This is not a vibes-based problem statement; "a datasheet value cannot represent this delivery" is an evidenced claim.

2. **The product's core stance — a delivery is an input, not a verdict — is a real change in outcome for the user.** "It does not reject the delivery. A dirty, soft or mixed batch is an input, not an ending" ([README.md:9](../README.md#L9)); "It will not say 'don't use this delivery'" — instead it states the required material range, the gap, and the levers: steadier stream, sort/wash/dry, change section or span, reroute to another product, split ([README.md:40–42](../README.md#L40-L42)). The mechanics implements this, it is not just copy: full-swing ranges must sit inside *every* target simultaneously ([app/product.js:326–344](../app/product.js#L326-L344), [app/product.js:554–557](../app/product.js#L554-L557)), the material gap is computed per requirement ([app/product.js:484–519](../app/product.js#L484-L519)), a blend search finds the smallest passing steadier-stream fraction ([app/product.js:541–552](../app/product.js#L541-L552)), and rerouting to the shop's other product is a first-class move ([app/product.js:912–913](../app/product.js#L912-L913)).

3. **The output is a shop-floor artefact, not a dashboard.** The decision is one headline ("Make the…", "Blend 65% of…, then make the…", "Lift the strength low end by…", [app/product.js:875–908](../app/product.js#L875-L908)) and the Copy build ticket carries plastic, batch, mix/dirt, blend %, every requirement result, added material, and move ([app/product.js:919–931](../app/product.js#L919-L931), contract in [V0_8_SPEC.md:103–106](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L103-L106)). A small shop can act on this the same morning a delivery lands. That is the correct shape of impact for this user.

4. **The benefit repeats.** Deliveries recur; each new one re-poses the question. The local mechanics works with no server ([README.md:71](../README.md#L71)), there are no browser runtime dependencies ([README.md:103](../README.md#L103)), and the typed/spoken brief lowers the engineering-literacy barrier to entry ([HACKATHON.md:16–22](../HACKATHON.md#L16-L22)). Repeat-use potential is structural, not hypothetical.

5. **Honesty protects the impact claim instead of inflating it.** The demo batch is labelled an assembled published-data example, not one measured delivery ([HACKATHON.md:50–56](../HACKATHON.md#L50-L56)); the planter is labelled illustrative ([data/CASE_STUDY.md:41–46](../data/CASE_STUDY.md#L41-L46)); no certification, food-contact, or physical blend test is claimed ([README.md:129–134](../README.md#L129-L134)). A judge can trust the impact that *is* claimed. (This same honesty is what makes the gaps below so visible — the team has not papered over them.)

Secondary beneficiaries are plausible but currently unquantified, and I credit them only as direction: the shop's customers get parts that hold their range across a swing, and a delivery that would have been rejected or downcycled gets used. The app itself claims neither, correctly.

## Track verdict: Build for Your World yes; Push the Frontier no

**Keep *Build for Your World*.** The claimed track ([HACKATHON.md:3](../HACKATHON.md#L3), [DEMO_SCRIPT.md:5](../DEMO_SCRIPT.md#L5)) fits precisely: a named kind of small shop, in the builder's own world of making things, with a concrete recurring problem and a tool shaped to that shop's morning workflow. This is the strongest available framing and switching would lose points.

***Push the Frontier* is not defensible as a primary or co-equal claim, and I would score it down if used.** What is live in v0.8 is textbook rectangular-section mechanics — cantilever `σ·b·t²/(6L)`, simply-supported `2σ·b·t²/(3L)`, compression squash/Euler ([V0_8_SPEC.md:55–60](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L55-L60), implemented [app/product.js:288–311](../app/product.js#L288-L311)) — plus a linear mixture rule for blends ([app/product.js:313–324](../app/product.js#L313-L324)). The genuinely frontier-flavoured material in the repo (reliability-based design optimisation, lattice variability dilution, bivariate-lognormal failure probability) is in the research dossiers and the retired bracket engine, which the submission itself explicitly places outside the judged surface and does not claim ([HACKATHON.md:73–75](../HACKATHON.md#L73-L75), [README.md:126–127](../README.md#L126-L127)). Claiming Frontier would invite comparison against novel methods and the submission's own disclaimer would be used against it. The verifier-gated model loop (Meta proposes, deterministic mechanics disposes) is a credible *application-pattern* novelty and is already the right secondary line — it belongs under Build for Your World's "real-world use of Meta models" argument ([HACKATHON.md:109–114](../HACKATHON.md#L109-L114)), not as a track change. One sentence in `HACKATHON.md` — "we claim Build for Your World; the frontier element is the verified-AI pattern, not new mechanics" — would pre-empt the question entirely.

## Is the user credible as presented?

**As a persona: yes. As a demonstrated user: no evidence exists, and the persona has a load-bearing prerequisite the product does not help with.**

- The audience is well drawn: "A small shop that knows its part and its target range, has measured its delivery, and can change the section, span, load path, product, or material feed" ([V0_8_SPEC.md:13–15](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L13-L15)). The segment logic is independently supported by the landscape dossier: incumbent tools are quote-only, expert-deployed CAE priced for Tier-1 programmes, and no product couples lot distribution → design decision for non-CAE users ([research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md:89–98](../research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md#L89-L98)). Note, however, that dossier's beachheads are recycled-filament producers, print farms, and furniture brands ([research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md:121–125](../research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md#L121-L125)) for a different, earlier product shape — it supports "underserved segment exists," not "this shop exists."
- **No beneficiary contact is documented anywhere.** I searched the judged docs (`README`, `HACKATHON`, `V0_8_SPEC`, `CASE_STUDY`, `DEMO_SCRIPT`, `SUBMISSION`) for an interview, pilot, named or anonymised shop, user test, or field visit: there is none. The crate shop in the demo narration ([DEMO_SCRIPT.md:20–23](../DEMO_SCRIPT.md#L20-L23)) is a narrative device. For the highest-weighted criterion, "who benefits" currently rests entirely on plausibility.
- **The input prerequisite quietly narrows who can benefit.** The app requires measured stiffness low/high and strength low/high in MPa ([app/index.html:112–119](../app/index.html#L112-L119)), and `isReady` refuses to decide without them ([app/product.js:227–243](../app/product.js#L227-L243)). Yet the same spec says the tool "does not require a lab" ([V0_8_SPEC.md:14–15](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L14-L15)). Where does a small shop get a *range* in MPa? The product never says: no supplier-certificate path, no single-value-plus-declared-swing entry, no published-range picker. The repo even owns a literature-derived feedstock library ([data/feedstock_library.json](../data/feedstock_library.json)) that the active app never references — I verified no mention of it in `app/`, `README.md`, `HACKATHON.md`, or `V0_8_SPEC.md`. As shipped, the beneficiary is "a shop that already receives or pays for tensile ranges," which is a much smaller and more sophisticated user than the pitch describes.
- **The breadth of the invitation exceeds the mechanics.** "What do you want to make?" accepts any product name ([app/index.html:19](../app/index.html#L19)), but any name is silently mapped to one of three load paths ([app/product.js:117–122](../app/product.js#L117-L122)) and one rectangular-section model ([HACKATHON.md:101–102](../HACKATHON.md#L101-L102)). That limit is honestly stated in the docs; it is not stated on the page, where a shop could type a part the model cannot represent and receive a confident headline.

## Does the app surface the consequence, or just numbers?

Mostly numbers. Audit of each claimed consequence:

| Consequence | In the docs | In the product itself |
|---|---|---|
| Batch swing → part out of range | Problem statement ([HACKATHON.md:9–12](../HACKATHON.md#L9-L12)) | **Yes, this is the core loop.** Calculated range vs every target ([app/product.js:326–344](../app/product.js#L326-L344)); headline + support state the ranges it holds ([app/product.js:909–918](../app/product.js#L909-L918)). |
| Rejected / wasted delivery | "rejecting the odd one is expensive" — asserted, unquantified, narration-only ([DEMO_SCRIPT.md:20–23](../DEMO_SCRIPT.md#L20-L23)) | **Only as an absence.** The app never rejects (by design), but it also never states the fate it averts: no delivery quantity anywhere in the UI, no "this delivery cannot make this part as-is" consequence line, no per-run exposure. A failing gap is expressed as material MPa needed vs given ([app/main.js:241–259](../app/main.js#L241-L259)) — the shop must translate MPa into "my parts would carry X kg against a 95 kg floor" itself. Notably, when no build passes, the current build's own failing property range (already computed as `currentRanges`, [app/product.js:741–742](../app/product.js#L741-L742)) is never shown; the builds list shows only passing builds ([app/main.js:180–213](../app/main.js#L180-L213)). |
| Over-design (the other failure mode) | Named once ([HACKATHON.md:10–11](../HACKATHON.md#L10-L11)) | **Partially, per-part only.** The winner is the least-added-mass passing build ([app/product.js:788–799](../app/product.js#L788-L799)), rows show `+X g` ([app/product.js:849–853](../app/product.js#L849-L853), [app/main.js:208–210](../app/main.js#L208-L210)), winner is badged "least build mass" ([app/main.js:196–200](../app/main.js#L196-L200)). But there is no baseline (what designing blind/to the worst lot would have cost) and no scaling: +12 g reads as abstract until multiplied by a run, and the app has no run-size concept. The avoided waste is computable from existing numbers and simply never computed. |
| Blend cost logic | Blend presented as a lever ([README.md:40–42](../README.md#L40-L42)) | **Missing, with a ranking flaw.** A blend is shown only as a percentage ([app/product.js:949](../app/product.js#L949), [app/product.js:929](../app/product.js#L929)). Worse: blending does not change density ([app/product.js:313–324](../app/product.js#L313-L324)), so a blend candidate's `addedG` is 0 ([app/product.js:765–783](../app/product.js#L765-L783)) and the winner sort by `addedG` ([app/product.js:793–798](../app/product.js#L793-L798)) treats consuming up to 90% steadier stream as free versus +30 g of section. The fraction itself is minimised for a given part ([app/product.js:545–551](../app/product.js#L545-L551)), which helps, but the app never shows grams of steadier stream per part, kg per run, stream availability, or any cost proxy — so it can recommend an operationally expensive recipe while labelling it the least-material answer. |
| Dirt / mix / moisture (the pain's drivers) | "dirt, mixed polymer and moisture move the low end further" ([HACKATHON.md:9–11](../HACKATHON.md#L9-L11)); Batch note: "Dirt tells us what to fix" ([app/index.html:101](../app/index.html#L101)) | **Collected but inert in the deterministic product.** Dirt level, dirt description, and mix are inputs ([app/index.html:103–111](../app/index.html#L103-L111)) that flow to normalisation, the copy ticket, and the Meta payloads ([app/product.js:923](../app/product.js#L923), [app/product.js:982–983](../app/product.js#L982-L983)) — but capacity, stiffness, mass, gap, and blend depend only on measured E/S ranges, density, and geometry ([app/product.js:288–324](../app/product.js#L288-L324)). The claim only holds if the shop has already folded dirt into its measured low end. That may be the intended contract, but neither the page nor the spec says so, and the section note promises more than the mechanics delivers. |

Also: the docs promise "split the delivery" as a lever ([README.md:42](../README.md#L42), [V0_8_SPEC.md:86–88](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L86-L88)), but the move set is `current / thickness / section / span / other / blend` ([app/product.js:55](../app/product.js#L55)) — split exists only as possible advice text, never as a computed option. ## Biggest credibility gaps (ranked by damage to an Impact score)

1. **Zero beneficiary evidence.** No shop, interview, pilot, or observed session. Everything about "who benefits and how much" is inference from a persona ([V0_8_SPEC.md:13–15](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L13-L15)). This is the single largest cap on the score at 30% weight.
2. **The flagship case is not a delivery.** Stiffness from one published lot, strength from a different published table, density from a standard range ([data/CASE_STUDY.md:21–24](../data/CASE_STUDY.md#L21-L24)), honestly labelled as assembled ([HACKATHON.md:52–56](../HACKATHON.md#L52-L56)) — but it means the demo proves "the arithmetic works on plausible published ranges," not "a real batch was rescued." Combined with gap 1, there is no point in the submission where a real material met a real part.
3. **The intended user may not be able to feed it.** Measured MPa low/high ranges are mandatory; no sourcing path, proxy, or library is offered despite the repo owning one (see above). If the real small shop cannot produce the inputs, the beneficiary collapses to shops that already have engineering support — the ones who need this least.
4. **Consequences are not on screen.** Waste averted, over-design avoided, and blend consumption are the entire economic impact, and none is quantified on screen (audit table above), including the blend-ranks-as-free flaw ([app/product.js:793–798](../app/product.js#L793-L798)). A judge cannot feel stakes the product does not state.
5. **The pain is invisible at first open and optional in the demo.** Blank question page ([app/index.html:19–20](../app/index.html#L19-L20)); the failure beat is a presenter-contrived edit two-thirds in ([DEMO_SCRIPT.md:58–68](../DEMO_SCRIPT.md#L58-L68)). If the presenter rushes, the judge sees only a case that already passes.
6. **The evidenced problem never enters the pitch.** The quantified swing evidence sits in `research/`, which the submission frames as pre-product history not claimed for v0.8 ([HACKATHON.md:73–75](../HACKATHON.md#L73-L75)), while the pitch's problem paragraph cites nothing ([HACKATHON.md:9–12](../HACKATHON.md#L9-L12)). A judge reading only submission docs gets assertion; the evidence is one directory away and disowned. (The research also carries regulatory demand context — recycled-content obligations, [research/01-recyclate-variability.md:37](../research/01-recyclate-variability.md#L37) — that the pitch never uses; usable only if scoped carefully to the parts actually modelled.)
7. **Repeat use has friction the product ignores.** No storage or saved state (by design, [app/README.md:7–9](../app/README.md#L7-L9)): every new delivery means re-entering product, targets, batch, and steadier stream. The recurring-use story in strength 4 above is undercut by the product's own amnesia.

## What a judge needs in 3 minutes to believe impact (without inventing anything)

1. **One evidenced sentence of pain with numbers, in the pitch itself** — e.g. the R01/R02 swing figures — replacing the current uncited assertion.
2. **The failing case as a first-class, precomputed case** (exact inputs, exact gap, exact rescue), not an instruction to improvise one, so the rescue is seen even in a rushed demo.
3. **A consequence line on screen in shop units**: what the current build actually carries vs the floor, and what the winner changes per part and per run.
4. **An honest beneficiary status line**: persona validated / not yet validated, stated as plainly as the mechanics limits already are.
5. **A believable answer to "where do the MPa ranges come from?"** visible in the Batch section.

None of these requires a customer, a market figure, or a deployment story. All are buildable from numbers and sources already in the repo.

## Ranked concrete fixes (product/doc only; no go-public or deployment advice)

1. **Put the consequence in the decision and gap, in part units.** In `decisionFor` / `renderGap` ([app/product.js:875–900](../app/product.js#L875-L900), [app/main.js:216–260](../app/main.js#L216-L260)): when no build passes, show the current build's calculated range against its target ("carries 78–95 kg; you need 95–115 kg") alongside the existing material-MPa gap — the value is already computed ([app/product.js:741–742](../app/product.js#L741-L742)) and discarded. When a build passes, state the change it makes ("+14 g per part vs your current section"). *Acceptance: a failing case reads as a shop consequence without opening the Batch maths.*
2. **Scale to a run and stop ranking blends as free.** Add one optional "parts in this run" field; multiply `addedG` ([app/product.js:849–853](../app/product.js#L849-L853)) into kg per run, and for blends show grams of steadier stream per part and kg per run (fraction × part mass, both known). Fix the winner ordering in `evaluateBuilds` ([app/product.js:788–799](../app/product.js#L788-L799)) so a blend carries its stream mass as its cost axis instead of `addedG = 0`, or present build-mass and stream-mass as two explicit columns in `proposalRow` ([app/product.js:935–953](../app/product.js#L935-L953)) and the copy ticket ([app/product.js:929](../app/product.js#L929)). *Acceptance: a 90%-stream blend can no longer beat a +20 g section on an invisible cost.*
3. **Give Batch a measurement on-ramp.** Wire the existing published ranges ([data/feedstock_library.json](../data/feedstock_library.json), case-study sources [data/CASE_STUDY.md:57–60](../data/CASE_STUDY.md#L57-L60)) into the Batch section as labelled starting ranges ("published example — replace with your supplier's range"), plus a single-value entry that derives low/high from a shop-declared swing %. State the input contract in one line where the fields are ([app/index.html:99–119](../app/index.html#L99-L119)) ("ranges from your supplier's certificate or your own measurements"). Amend the audience line ([V0_8_SPEC.md:13–15](../V0_8_SPEC.md#L13-L15)) to match. *Acceptance: a shop with only a supplier datasheet can reach a decision, with provenance visible.*
4. **Make the failure case a worked case.** In [data/CASE_STUDY.md:48–53](../data/CASE_STUDY.md#L48-L53), replace the instruction with exact inputs and the app's exact outputs (gap per requirement, rescue build or blend %), generated from the app/tests, and align the demo order so that case is reachable in one click (it already exists as a preset pattern, [app/product.js:16–48](../app/product.js#L16-L48)). Correct the closing overclaim at [HACKATHON.md:113–114](../HACKATHON.md#L113-L114) to describe what the demo actually shows. *Acceptance: the rejection-and-rescue beat survives a rushed 3-minute demo with numbers a judge can check.*
5. **Import the evidenced pain into the pitch, scoped honestly.** Add two cited figures from [research/01-recyclate-variability.md:29–31](../research/01-recyclate-variability.md#L29-L31) to [HACKATHON.md:9–12](../HACKATHON.md#L9-L12) with source IDs, framed as literature ranges, and add the beneficiary-status sentence ("persona from segment research; not yet validated with a named shop") next to the track claim ([HACKATHON.md:3–5](../HACKATHON.md#L3-L5)). Do **not** import the old product's beachheads, pricing, or multi-year figures ([research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md:119–142](../research/04-software-landscape-and-gap.md#L119-L142)) — they describe a different product and are labelled hypotheses there. *Acceptance: the problem paragraph is evidenced, and the user claim is exactly as strong as the evidence.*
6. **Make dirt/mix earn their fields or soften the claim.** Either key deterministic gap actions to `dirtLevel` in `materialGap` (wash/dry for moisture-linked levels, sort for mixed-polymer descriptions — the action slot already exists, [app/product.js:500–518](../app/product.js#L500-L518)), or change the Batch note ([app/index.html:101](../app/index.html#L101)) to state the true contract: "your measured low end should already reflect dirt and mix; note them here for the advice and the build ticket." Also reconcile the "split the delivery" lever ([README.md:42](../README.md#L42)) with the actual move set ([app/product.js:55](../app/product.js#L55)) — implement it as a computed option or remove it from the lever list. *Acceptance: every Batch field either changes a number, changes an action, or is labelled as record-only.*
7. **Scope the invitation on the page.** Add the rectangular-section / three-load-path qualifier to the Engineering note ([app/index.html:46–48](../app/index.html#L46-L48)) so "What do you want to make?" does not silently promise parts the mechanics cannot represent ([app/product.js:117–122](../app/product.js#L117-L122)). Narrower promise, higher belief.
8. **Remember the shop between deliveries.** Persist product, targets, steadier stream, and named batch ranges locally (the no-storage stance is documented at [app/README.md:7–9](../app/README.md#L7-L9); this changes product behaviour, so update that contract) so the second delivery starts from the shop's last state and the swing between deliveries can be shown side by side. *Acceptance: a returning shop reaches a decision by editing only the new delivery's ranges — the recurring-use claim becomes visible in the product.*

Fixes 1–4 are the ones that move the Impact score; 1 and 2 are pure presentation/accounting over numbers the engine already produces. With 1–5 done, I would expect this to score around 7/10 on Impact without any new customer claims. The remaining distance to a higher score is not presentation — it is one real shop's delivery run through the product, which no document edit can substitute for.
